**1. Transaction at a Glance**

Deal value, price per share, premium to last close and 30-day VWAP, net

consideration after cash, expected close, regulatory conditions, and

financial accretion guidance (revenue, operating profit, EPS — with

timeline for each).

**2. Acquirer Context and Strategic Pressure**

Who is the acquirer? What are their core historical business areas,

revenue pillars, and identity? What strategic pressures — patent cliffs,

LOE risk, growth gaps, competitive threats — are driving them to transact?

How mature or nascent is their presence in the target's domain? What is

the acquirer's stated M&A philosophy and how does this deal fit it?

Include any relevant CEO or leadership transition context that shapes

the strategic intent. Distinguish between established pillars and

emerging focus areas.

**3. Target Description**

What does the target company do? When was it founded, by whom, and

on what scientific or technological thesis? What is the core modality

or platform — and is it biologics, small molecule, gene therapy,

or platform technology? Is this a compound company or a repeatable

discovery engine?

**4. Scientific or Technical Education**

For a sophisticated but non-specialist executive audience, explain the

underlying science needed to evaluate this deal. If the target operates

in a field with generational evolution (e.g., successive waves of

resistance, platform iterations, mechanism classes), map that landscape

explicitly. Distinguish between related but distinct sub-categories

where they exist. Flag where simplification risks losing important nuance.

**5. Pipeline Assets**

For each key asset: target/mechanism, indication(s), current clinical

stage, regulatory status and key dates, clinical trial names and

enrollment, designations (Breakthrough, Orphan, Fast Track), and

the specific differentiation vs. current standard of care. Be precise

about what label/indication is currently filed vs. what is aspirational

through ongoing trials.

**6. Competitive Differentiation**

What gap does the target's portfolio address that existing approved

or late-stage therapies do not? Is this a best-in-class, first-in-class,

or next-generation positioning? What are the primary comparators and

on which dimensions does the target win or lose? Be specific —

differentiation claims must be tied to mechanism or clinical data,

not marketing language.

**7. Strategic Alignment and Franchise Logic**

How do the target's assets fit into the acquirer's existing portfolio,

therapeutic area strategy, and pipeline? Map the intended treatment

architecture — what does the combined company's patient journey look

like across the indication(s)? What does the acquirer gain that it

could not have built organically in the same timeframe? What

complementary assets (already owned) does this acquisition unlock

or accelerate?

**8. Combination or Synergy Rationale**

If the strategic thesis involves combining the target's assets with

existing acquirer programs (e.g., mechanism-complementary therapies,

sequential or concurrent treatment strategies), explain the scientific

or commercial logic explicitly. Distinguish between combinations that

have clinical precedent vs. those that are scientifically sound but

as-yet unproven. Do not overstate the established-ness of emerging

strategies.

**9. Market and Commercial Opportunity**

Patient population size and characteristics. Pricing precedent and

payer dynamics. Competitive intensity in the commercial landscape.

Multi-blockbuster potential or niche orphan positioning. Clearly flag

any figures that are analyst estimates rather than sourced data.

**10. Implications for [YOUR COMPANY TYPE]**

Assess what this transaction means specifically for [COMPANY TYPE].

Distinguish between direct implications (the target's assets create

demand for your services/products) and indirect implications (the

acquirer's broader strategy creates downstream opportunity). Identify

what capabilities would be required to be a relevant partner. Flag

where opportunity is near-term vs. speculative.

**11. Three to Five Strategic Watch Items**

What specific events, data readouts, trial initiations, regulatory

decisions, or partnership announcements should be monitored over

the next 12–24 months, and why does each matter to your organization?

---

**Format:** Executive memo, [X] words. Written for a CEO with strong

[industry] fluency but not necessarily deep [scientific domain] expertise.

Action-oriented framing. Avoid marketing language — every differentiation

claim must be tied to a mechanism, clinical data point, or sourced fact.

**CRITICAL INSTRUCTIONS ON ACCURACY:**

Before finalizing your response, conduct an internal audit of every

factual claim. Apply the following sourcing hierarchy:

- SOURCED FACT: Claim directly supported by a press release, SEC filing,

  FDA document, or peer-reviewed publication. State these with confidence.

- ANALYST ESTIMATE: Claim derived from inference, calculation, or

  training knowledge not verified against a primary source in this

  session. Flag these explicitly with "[Analyst estimate — verify]".

- CHARACTERIZATION: Strategic interpretation or comparative judgment

  (e.g., "Company X's manufacturing is less established than Company Y's").

  Flag as "[Analytical opinion — not sourced]".

Specific accuracy rules:

- Approval dates, PDUFA dates, clinical enrollment numbers, and deal

  terms must be sourced. Do not state from memory.

- Market size patient volumes derived from percentage prevalence figures

  must show the calculation and flag the underlying incidence assumption.

- Generational or competitive landscapes (e.g., drug generations,

  approved competitive products) must be complete — omitting an approved

  competitor is a material error.

- Where an analogy is used to establish precedent (e.g., "this strategy

  is proven by X"), verify the analogy is mechanistically accurate,

  not just thematically similar.

- Distinguish between what the acquirer's current regulatory filings

  cover vs. what is aspirational through ongoing clinical programs.
